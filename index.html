Bio

Kate Revoredo is a Senior Researcher at the Chair of Process Management and Information Systems at the Department of Computer Science, Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin, Germany. Her main research interest is data-centric approaches for information systems, spanning business process management, data science, semantic web, and knowledge representation.

Kate obtained her habilitation (Privatdozentin) in Information Systems from the Vienna University of Economics and Business (WU Vienna). She earned her PhD in Computer Science and Engineering from the Federal University of Rio de Janeiro, where her thesis on probabilistic machine learning received national recognition. She has worked at several universities including WU Vienna, USP, UNIRIO, and UFRJ, and has experience in applied research from collaborations including Petrobras and the Brazilian Navy.

Her research interests include process mining and business process intelligence, predictive process monitoring, knowledge representation and integration, and data science.

Core Research Areas

Process Mining & Predictive Process Monitoring

My work in this area focuses on data-driven analysis and prediction of business processes: fairness and bias in process mining (conceptualizing biases across the process mining lifecycle and the interventions to mitigate them, as in FairPM); process model repair using domain knowledge and theory revision; and predictive and context-aware process monitoring, including event-case correlation, process performance indicator prediction, and next-activity prediction. I am particularly interested in making process mining outcomes more accurate, explainable, and equitable.

Knowledge Representation & Ontology Matching

My doctoral and ongoing work addresses knowledge representation and integration: probabilistic description logics and theory revision for learning and repairing ontologies; and interactive ontology matching, developed through the Alin system, a long-running participant in the Ontology Alignment Evaluation Initiative (OAEI). This work connects data-centric information systems to semantic web and knowledge graph techniques.
